The Boring 80% of Cold Email No One Posts About

June 2026 · 4 min read

Scroll through LinkedIn and cold email content is almost entirely about the 20% that's fun to talk about: subject lines, personalisation hooks, the perfect three-line opener. Nobody posts about domain records. Nobody writes a carousel about mailbox warm-up schedules. And yet that unglamorous 80% is exactly what decides whether the clever 20% ever gets read.

I'm in the middle of that 80% right now. We're standing up a new cold email motion on Instantly at Workhall, and I've taken ownership of it end to end — domain creation, an interim educative landing page, mailbox setup, and warm-up, before a single sequence goes live. Here's what that actually looks like from the inside.

What "Setting Up a Campaign" Really Means

Before any prospect ever sees a subject line, there's a stack of infrastructure decisions that have to happen in the right order. Skip a step, or rush one, and the campaign is compromised before it starts — no amount of good copy fixes a domain that mailbox providers don't trust yet.

1
Domain creation and separationNew sending domains — kept separate from the primary company domain — so that if anything goes wrong early on, it never touches the domain we rely on for everything else.
2
An interim, educative landing pageBefore any outreach goes out, the domain needs somewhere real to point to. I built a simple landing page that actually informs a visitor — not a placeholder — because a thin or missing destination is exactly the kind of signal that damages trust with both recipients and mailbox providers.
3
Mailbox setupCreating the individual mailboxes that will actually send the sequences, and making sure each one is configured correctly from day one rather than patched later.
4
Warm-up, patientlyEvery mailbox needs to build a sending history before it can be trusted with real volume. That means starting small and letting the ramp-up run its course, even when the instinct is to speed things along.

Why the Landing Page Came Before the Emails

It would have been faster to skip the landing page and just start sending. But think about it from the other side: a cold email that links to nothing, or to a page that looks unfinished, reads as a red flag — to a curious prospect and, just as importantly, to spam filters evaluating where that domain's traffic actually leads. Building something genuinely useful first, even a simple one, is part of earning the right to send.

Why Warm-Up Can't Be Rushed

The hardest part of this stage isn't technical, it's psychological. There's always pressure to get campaigns live faster, and warm-up is the easiest thing to want to shortcut. But a mailbox with no sending history that suddenly sends fifty emails a day looks exactly like a spam operation to a mail server — regardless of how good the messaging is. Slow and boring, in this one specific place, is the correct strategy.

Nobody writes a LinkedIn post about their DKIM records. But every reply you'll ever get starts there.

Where I am right now

This build is still in progress — domains and mailboxes are set up, the landing page is live, and warm-up is running. The sequences and copy come next. I'll be sharing what happens once we flip this on, but the lesson so far holds regardless of the outcome: the unglamorous setup work isn't a delay before the real work starts. It is the real work.
